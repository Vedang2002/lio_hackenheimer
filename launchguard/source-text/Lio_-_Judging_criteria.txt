01
A pitch that sticks
Five teams later, the jury should still remember yours. Funny, wild or deadly serious: your call.
WHAT WE ASK OURSELVES
?
Do we understand the problem in the first 30 seconds?
?
Is there a clear line from what hurts at Soony to what you built?
?
Did you use what you learned from Soonyʼs team, not just from the data?
STRONG
A story with a real moment in it: “On Saturday,

WEAK
A feature tour. Ten slides of architecture before we

02
A real business case
Soony is your first customer, not your only one. Show us a company, not a one-off project.
WHAT WE ASK OURSELVES
?
Is it well motivated: why this problem, why now, and what is it worth in money or time?
?
Why would Soony pay for it, and roughly how much?
?
Who else has this problem? Does it work beyond one game launch?
STRONG
A number the customer cares about, and a clear
path to the next ten customers.
WEAK
“The market is huge.ˮ A solution that only works
for this one weekend.

 IN DETAIL
03
Technical depth
The one rule of the challenge: agents do real work. They read, decide and act, on data that is messy and
keeps changing.
WHAT WE ASK OURSELVES
?
Do your agents take real actions (decide, write, escalate), or do they only summarize?
?
Is there a solid architecture: tool use, reasoning, state, not one big prompt?
?
How do you handle messy and contradictory data? Is anything hardcoded?
?
When a new drop arrives, does your system cope, or do you rebuild it?
STRONG
Agents that take real actions on the messy data.
The team can explain what happens when the
system gets something wrong.
WEAK
A chat window around one prompt. Values copied
from the data into the code.
04
Taste
Soonyʼs team has ten minutes and forty dashboards. Build something that looks and feels like a product
they actually want to open.
WHAT WE ASK OURSELVES
?
Does it look and feel like a real product?
?
Is it all of a piece: one design language, a clear layout, nothing that feels bolted on?
?
Would the people you interviewed use this on a stressful Sunday?
?
Are the small details right: wording, defaults, the moment a human has to decide?
STRONG
It feels like one product: a clean, consistent
design, a clear recommendation and one click to
act.
WEAK
Every number on one screen, mixed styles and
default components. The user has to figure out
what to do.

Tips from the jury
Show, donʼt tell.
 Start the demo early. Slides are support, not the show.
Have a backup.
 If the Wi-Fi dies, your demo video saves you. Still, the live run is what counts.
Go deep, not wide.
 One problem solved properly beats a tour of everything.
